Supplementary Figure S1: Chronic tubulointerstitial injury in mouse models.

Representative hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of kidney sections from male

C57BL/6NCrl control mice (n=14) (A) and mice after 4 weeks fed an adenine-enriched diet

(tubulointerstitial nephritis, TIN, n=11) (B), 10 days after unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO,

n=10) (C), and 14 days after bilateral renal ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury (n=10) (D). Scale

bars represent 100 µm.



Supplementary Figure S2: Semiautomatic segmentation of peritubular capillaries in

combination with manual post-processing.

(A, D) Original immunofluorescence images of MECA-32-stained peritubular capillaries in

mouse kidney cortex.

(B, E) Capillary masks after semiautomatic segmentation in Fiji/ImageJ using the algorithm

shown in Figure 1. Irregular and insufficient staining intensity of the capillary endothelium can

lead to incomplete segmentation.

(C, F) Capillary masks were manually closed and/or connected as needed before

morphometric analysis.

Scale bars represent 15 µm.



Supplementary Figure 3: Roundness and circularity of unprocessed peritubular

capillaries in mouse models with chronic tubulointerstitial injury.

Relative frequency distribution of all roundness (A, C, E, G) and circularity (B, D, F, H) values

of peritubular capillaries in male C57BL/6NCrl control mice (n=14) (A, B) and mice with

adenine-induced tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN, n=11) (C, D), unilateral ureteral obstruction

(UUO, n=10) (E, F) and bilateral ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury (n=10) (G, H).

Semiautomatically segmented, unprocessed masks were analyzed. Indicated are the number

(N) of capillaries, mean, median, mode, standard deviation (SD) and interquartile range (IQR)

of distribution. Bar width represents ranges of 0.05.
Supplementary Figure S4: Co-localization of peritubular capillaries with distinct renal

tubule segments in mouse kidneys.

Kidney sections from male C57BL/6NCrl control mice (A, C, E) and mice with adenine-induced

tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN) (B, D, F) were co-stained with antibodies against MECA-32

and megalin (marker for proximal tubule) (A, B), sodium chloride cotransporter (NCC, marker

for distal convoluted tubule) (C, D) or 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11βHSD2,

marker for distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct) (E, F).

Scale bars represent 50 µm.



Supplementary Figure S5: Capillary tortuosity in control mouse kidneys and chronic

tubulointerstitial nephritis.

Three-dimensional (3D) skeletons of kidney cortex microvasculature were generated using

light sheet fluorescence microscopy in male C57BL/6NCrl control mice (n=4) (A) and mice with

adenine-induced tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN, n=4) (B). Per animal 12-31 3D vascular

skeletons were analyzed.

Relative frequency distribution of mean capillary segment tortuosity per 3D vascular skeleton.

Indicated are the number (N) of 3D skeletons, mean, median, mode, standard deviation (SD),

and interquartile range (IQR) of capillary segment tortuosity. Bar width represents ranges of

0.00125.



Supplementary Figure S6: Chronic tubulointerstitial injury and microvascular

alterations in human kidney biopsies.

Human kidney biopsies from patients with IgA nephropathy (A-D) and from time-zero renal

allograft (E, F) were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) (A, C, E) and

immunohistochemically for CD31 (B, D, F). Images show mild (A, B), moderate (C, D) and

severe (E, F) chronic tubulointerstitial injury and alterations of the renal microvasculature.

Scale bars represent 200 µm.
Supplementary Figure S7: Adequacy of the segmentation algorithm.

Peritubular capillaries were stained for MECA-32 (A, D), segmented semiautomatically and

post-processed manually (B, E), as shown in Figure 1 and Supplementary Figure S2. To

assess the adequacy of the segmentation algorithm, outlines (yellow) of the segmented masks

were overlaid on the original immunofluorescence images (C, F).

Scale bars represent 50 µm.



Supplementary Figure S8: Parameters of morphometric analysis.

Peritubular capillaries were segmented semiautomatically using the algorithm in Figure 1 and

evaluated morphometrically using the “Analyze Particles” command in Fiji/ImageJ. The

depicted parameters were measured.

Modified from: https://imagej.net/plugins/biovoxxel-toolbox, retrieved August 7, 2021.

Scale bar represents 15 µm.
