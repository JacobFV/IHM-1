# Digestion, substrate control, and whole-body coupling audit

Date: 2026-09-05. Read-only inspection of retained BioGears commit `3f16a5fa1dade9c511b88d923606fa51cc35e95d`, the current native adapter, and existing native XML states. No donor physiology or adapter code was changed for this audit; no new meal simulation was run. The recommendations below are implementation candidates, not demonstrated experiment results.

## Finding

The retained native engine contains an executable causal meal-to-body chain. `SEConsumeNutrients` adds a meal to native stomach storage; native digestion transfers substrate to a single small-intestine chyme compartment; native absorption transfers mass to small-intestine vasculature; blood transport, endocrine secretion, hepatic storage/conversion, tissue metabolism, and renal handling then operate on those same substance quantities. Extending the adapter to expose this chain is substantially more defensible than building a second nutrition model.

This is a lumped substrate-control model, not a mechanically resolved alimentary tract. There are material source defects and missing mechanisms. In particular, existing saved output already contains negative stomach calcium. A successful meal action or plausible glucose curve alone cannot establish nutrient mass conservation or broad whole-body realism.

## Source map and actual laws

All paths in this section are relative to `data/raw/physiology/biogears/`. Source execution is authoritative when prose documentation differs.

| Causal link | Retained source and behavior |
|---|---|
| Ingestion | `projects/biogears/libBiogears/src/engine/Systems/Gastrointestinal.cpp:276`: active `PreProcess` loads optional nutrition XML, increments `m_StomachContents`, increments patient weight by meal weight, removes the one-shot action, and digests on the same step. This bypasses chewing, swallowing, and esophageal transport. |
| Stomach to chyme | `Gastrointestinal.cpp:331`: Michaelis–Menten form `Vmax*C/(Km+C)`, with concentration calculated using a fixed 1000 mL assumed stomach volume. Carbohydrate becomes Glucose, protein becomes AminoAcids, fat becomes Triacylglycerol. These are equal-mass transfers between source reservoirs, not detailed hydrolysis chemistry. |
| Intestinal secretion and absorption | `Gastrointestinal.cpp:480,640`: sodium secretion from intestine blood to chyme has maximum 5 g/h, half-saturation 0.2 g. Nutrient-dependent sodium absorption has maximum 9 g/h, half-saturation 2 g. Glucose transfer is twice the computed sodium mass; amino-acid transfer equals it, in separate branches that each also move sodium. TAG moves directly to blood at 1.9 mg/s. Fluid absorption is `LogisticFunction(13,500,0.007,chyme_mL)` mL/min and acts through a cardiovascular circuit flow source. |
| Portal transport | `src/engine/Controller/BioGears.cpp:2135`: `SmallIntestineVasculature` links directly to `LiverVasculature`; its path maps to `SmallIntestineToPortalVein`. Portal vein is explicitly a path, not a separately resolved portal blood reservoir; its pressure difference is not physiologic portal pressure (`:1246,1276`). |
| Endocrine feedback | `Systems/Endocrine.cpp:167,232`: aortic glucose concentration drives fitted insulin and glucagon synthesis functions. Production rates are pmol/min; conversion by molar mass and native timestep increments hormone mass in `SplanchnicVasculature`, which drains to liver. Diabetes conditions modify production and hepatic hormone interpretation. Insulin synthesis alone is not circulating insulin concentration. |
| Hepatic storage and conversion | `Systems/Hepatic.cpp:171`: sequential glycogenesis, lipogenesis, glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis. `:218,292` move glucose between vascular masses and **Tissue-owned** liver/muscle glycogen scalars, controlled by relative local insulin/glucagon deviations. `:326` converts lactate, TAG-derived glycerol, and amino acids into glucose/ketones/urea using source-specific approximations; `:534` converts glucose/amino acids to TAG. |
| Tissue stores and energy | `Systems/Tissue.cpp:520,889`: protein/fat storage and release, tissue substrate consumption, O2 consumption/CO2 production, lactate, pulmonary transfer and diffusion. Demand comes from basal metabolism, exercise, temperature-related demand, and biological debt. Glucose oxidation uses 6 O2 and 6 CO2 per glucose with an ATP-yield approximation; substrate shortage produces native fatigue/achievement responses. Stores are scalar state, not independent organs to add again to blood/tissue mass. |
| Heat and energy control | `Systems/Energy.cpp:200,220,540,834`: source exercise and thermal demand control total metabolic rate; the thermal circuit receives metabolic heat. A meal does not directly inject calories into a heat store. Tissue selects/consumes available substrates to meet demand. `Tissue.cpp:899` explicitly uses a scaled “faux” hypoperfusion energy-deficit signal, so this is not a universal first-law energy ledger. |
| Renal handling | `Systems/Renal.cpp:738,976,1020,1135`: filtration, reabsorption, secretion, transport and excretion operate on individual substances; renal lactate-to-glucose conversion is separately approximated (`:637`). Urine glucose, sodium, urea and concentrations can be observed. Glomerular filtration alone is not excreted nutrient mass. |

The official [GI methodology](https://www.biogearsengine.com/documentation/_gastrointestinal_methodology.html) describes the lumping into glucose, alanine-like amino acids and tripalmitin-like TAG, and identifies intestinal lymph transport as future work. Its prose also describes gastric secretion, but the retained active call `GastricSecretion(m_dT_s)` is commented out at `Gastrointestinal.cpp:293`. The [Tissue methodology](https://www.biogearsengine.com/documentation/_tissue_methodology.html) acknowledges incompletely characterized chemical reactions. Neither document supersedes the inspected revision.

## Public API and proposed adapter extension

The present `scripts/native_biogears_rest.cpp` supports only three-column scalar events (`exercise`, `hemorrhage`, `saline`), advances at 0.02 s, and caps a run at 3600 s. It reports insulin synthesis and total metabolic power but does not expose stomach/intestinal substrate masses or the four nutrient stores.

Add a structured meal event with explicit `time_s`, `name`, `carbohydrate_g`, `protein_g`, `fat_g`, `sodium_g`, `calcium_mg`, and `water_mL`. Do not silently reinterpret its existing scalar event value as calories. Validate all fields as finite and nonnegative, require a positive total meal mass and an explicit name, and align event time to the native clock. The actual public action is:

```cpp
#include <biogears/cdm/patient/actions/SEConsumeNutrients.h>
SEConsumeNutrients meal;
auto& nutrition = meal.GetNutrition();
nutrition.SetName("mixed_meal");
nutrition.GetCarbohydrate().SetValue(60.0, MassUnit::g);
nutrition.GetProtein().SetValue(20.0, MassUnit::g);
nutrition.GetFat().SetValue(20.0, MassUnit::g);
nutrition.GetSodium().SetValue(1.0, MassUnit::g);
nutrition.GetCalcium().SetValue(0.0, MassUnit::mg);
nutrition.GetWater().SetValue(500.0, VolumeUnit::mL);
if (!bg->ProcessAction(meal)) /* fail the run */;
```

This is source-backed API pseudocode, not a compiled patch. `SENutrition::IsValid()` requires a name and at least one supported nutrient (`src/cdm/patient/SENutrition.cpp:300`); setting all six fields avoids absent-scalar ambiguity. `SetNutritionFile()` is an alternative. Meal weight sums the five mass fields plus water at 1 g/mL (`:281`). No separate calorie input, fiber, potassium, chloride, vitamin, amino-acid spectrum, fatty-acid spectrum, food texture, digestibility, or nutrient-specific transit-rate field exists in `SENutrition.h`.

Expose the following read-only native telemetry in a dedicated nutrient CSV with units and source identifiers. Use `Has...` checks and reject unexpected missing/nonfinite state rather than substituting zero. `PhysiologyEngine` exposes const system pointers; getters with explicit unit arguments preserve read-only observation.

| Quantity | Exact getter route / unit |
|---|---|
| Stomach contents | `bg->GetGastrointestinalSystem()->GetStomachContents()` returns `const SENutrition*`; its `GetCarbohydrate(MassUnit::g)`, `GetProtein(g)`, `GetFat(g)`, `GetSodium(g)`, `GetCalcium(mg)`, `GetWater(VolumeUnit::mL)` |
| Fluid absorption | `GetGastrointestinalSystem()->GetChymeAbsorptionRate(VolumePerTimeUnit::mL_Per_min)`; this is water flow, **not** glucose mass flux |
| Nutrient reservoirs | `bg->GetCompartments().GetLiquidCompartment(name)->GetSubstanceQuantity(*substance)->GetMass(MassUnit::g)` and `GetConcentration(MassPerVolumeUnit::mg_Per_dL)`; obtain substance via `bg->GetSubstanceManager().GetSubstance("Glucose")`, also `AminoAcids`, `Triacylglycerol`, `Sodium`, `Calcium`, `Lactate`, `Urea`, `Ketones`, `Insulin`, `Glucagon` as present |
| Compartment identities | `SmallIntestineChyme`, `SmallIntestineVasculature`, `SplanchnicVasculature`, `LiverVasculature`, `LiverTissueExtracellular`, `Aorta`, `MuscleVasculature`, `MuscleTissueIntracellular`; observe `Bladder`, `LeftTubules`, `RightTubules` separately for renal handling |
| Hormone concentrations | Insulin `GetMolarity(AmountPerVolumeUnit::mmol_Per_L)`; report this native unit or multiply by 1e9 for pmol/L. Glucagon `GetConcentration(MassPerVolumeUnit::mg_Per_mL)`; multiply by 1e9 for pg/mL. Record conversions as presentation metadata. |

| Whole-body metabolism | `bg->GetTissueSystem()->GetOxygenConsumptionRate(VolumePerTimeUnit::mL_Per_min)`, `GetCarbonDioxideProductionRate(...)`, `GetRespiratoryExchangeRatio()`; `bg->GetEnergySystem()->GetTotalMetabolicRate(PowerUnit::W)`, `GetEnergyDeficit(PowerUnit::W)`, `GetFatigueLevel()`, `GetAchievedExerciseLevel()` |
| Renal substrate removal | `substance->GetClearance().GetRenalExcretionRate(MassPerTimeUnit::mg_Per_s)` and `GetRenalReabsorptionRate(...)`; kidney intracellular substance `GetMassExcreted(MassUnit::mg)` is a cumulative accounting diagnostic, not an additional physical mass reservoir |
| Urinary substrates and fluid | For `Bladder`, `LeftTubules`, `RightTubules`, `LeftUreter`, `RightUreter`, use the substance route above for `Glucose`, `Urea`, `Sodium`, `Lactate` mass (mg) and concentration (mg/mL). `bg->GetRenalSystem()->GetUrineVolume(VolumeUnit::mL)`, `GetUrineProductionRate(VolumePerTimeUnit::mL_Per_min)`, `GetUrinationRate(...)`, `GetUrineUreaNitrogenConcentration(MassPerVolumeUnit::g_Per_L)` distinguish storage, production, voiding, and nitrogen concentration. |

Keep physiological CSV data-request names source-defined; nested stomach values are safest through getters. No claim is made that every getter route is currently wired into IHM's bindings.

The inspected canonical `native_baseline_v1/states/native_stabilized.xml` has `NoMacros` stomach contents: 0 g carbohydrate/protein/fat, **500 mg calcium, 1 g sodium, 500 mL water**. Its liver glycogen is 117 g, muscle glycogen 592.199946 g, stored protein 109.996302 g, and stored fat 16298.516173 g. “No meal action” therefore does not mean an empty gut, depleted stores, or a defined overnight fast. Existing chyme/blood/tissue state must also be recorded.

Branch meal/control experiments from the same exact state file, source revision, compiled variant, patient, timestep and environment; retain hashes. ## Defects and missing causal links that affect claims

1. **Digestion rate units are inconsistent.** The three Vmax variables are named mg/min, consistent with the methodology table, but `Gastrointestinal.cpp:365–367` multiplies by `m_dT_s`; the `/60` conversion is commented out. Executed maximum rates are effectively 7.11, 7.1 and 3.4 mg/s. Preserve this behavior under an upstream label; any correction needs a separately versioned variant and comparison. Merely adding `/60` changes meal digestion by roughly sixtyfold and is not automatically a validated physiological improvement.
2. **Calcium loses mass and can go negative.** `Gastrointestinal.cpp:421` decrements a value calculated in mg using `MassUnit::g`, but credits chyme in mg. The remaining-mass comparison is performed before this 1000-fold discrepancy. It lacks a final remainder transfer. Existing `data/derived/physiology/native_hour_rest/states/native_final.xml` contains stomach calcium `-0.175000000020085 mg`; the baseline final file falls from 500 to 162.5 mg. Zero-calcium meals do not avoid the initial state's calcium defect.
3. **Renal transport maximum does not cap the transferred mass.** `Renal.cpp:1088` caps `reabsorptionRate_mg_Per_s`, but `:1105` still transfers the earlier uncapped `massToMove_mg`. Glucose XML specifies infinite reabsorption ratio and 0.375 g/min transport maximum. Consequently, the reported capped rate and actual transport can disagree, undermining a glucosuria or renal-threshold test until an explicit fix is validated.
4. **No nutrient transit through nine bowel segments.** `ProcessDrugCAT()` and its stomach/duodenum/jejunum/ileum/cecum/colon states are **drug-only** (`Gastrointestinal.cpp:807`). They do not carry meal carbohydrate, protein, fat, or fecal nutrient loss. Reusing their labels for meal animation would invent a causal path.
5. **No mechanically or neurally driven eating/digestion.** The path omits mastication, swallowing, gastric motility/accommodation/emptying feedback, peristalsis, enteric control, pancreatic enzyme secretion dynamics, bile secretion regulation, incretin/satiety hormones, microbiome/fiber fermentation, and fecal bulk/defecation. GI secretion is partly prescribed and gastric-water secretion is inactive. The native nervous system does not make these omitted GI mechanisms present.
6. **Fat enters blood directly.** A lymph TAG pointer exists, but active nutrient absorption increments small-intestine vascular TAG; it does not implement chylomicron assembly or a lacteal/lymph delay.
7. **Not a closed elemental or energetic chemistry model.** Equal-mass nutrient substitutions, incomplete reaction products, omitted lipogenesis ATP cost (`Hepatic.cpp:560`), simplified renal lactate conversion and faux energy deficit prevent claiming an exact atom/energy balance. Hepatic gluconeogenesis output is updated only below a 1000 g/day guard (`:478`), which can leave stale telemetry at high rates.

## Ownership and budget boundaries

The native engine must own stomach/chyme contents, vascular/extravascular concentrations, tissue stores, hormone secretion, renal removal and thermal/metabolic state. The adapter owns only event delivery, provenance, observations and clearly declared perturbation controls. The body view reads these states; it must not separately credit absorbed glucose, subtract meal energy, or update glycogen/fat in Python.

For a GI-only transfer ledger, track cumulative ingested carbohydrate/protein/fat plus initial stomach/chyme content against current stomach/chyme content and integrated **actual branch transfer to vasculature**. Same-step before/after branch mass increments can provide exact native transfer counters in an instrumented source variant. Ordinary sampled vascular mass differences cannot identify absorption because blood flow, tissue uptake and metabolism act concurrently. Sodium needs both vascular-to-chyme secretion and chyme-to-vascular absorption; water needs the circuit transfer; calcium currently fails this ledger. Do not infer glucose flux from water absorption rate.

For a systemic budget, sum a disjoint set of liquid leaf compartments, never parent plus child quantities (e.g. GutVasculature plus SmallIntestineVasculature or whole kidney plus nephron leaves). Include scalar glycogen/protein/fat stores once under their model-defined meaning. Chemical conversions require stoichiometric bookkeeping beyond raw glucose mass. Urine entering bladder is outside the circulating-body subsystem but still inside a whole-person-plus-bladder physical boundary; cumulative renal excretion is a diagnostic and must not be counted again as physical bladder mass. Boundary losses include actual voided urine, respiratory gases, sweat and other modeled sinks. An unexplained residual must be reported, not hidden in an “unmodeled metabolism” balancing term.

## Executable perturbation sequence and time horizons

These are numerical experiment horizons and test inputs, not claims about validated clinical transit times.

1. **Immediate ingestion/transfer smoke:** branch the same native state into no action, water-only, and the explicit 60 g carbohydrate / 20 g protein / 20 g fat / 1 g sodium / 500 mL meal above. Observe before the action and after its first advanced timestep. Assert one-time meal weight addition, native stomach accounting including first-step digestion, finite nonnegative substrate state, and no duplicate action at checkpoint replay. The existing calcium defect should be surfaced as a failure in the upstream profile.
2. **Absorption and endocrine response:** use continuous 4–6 h native runs at 0.02 s with 1 Hz or slower *output*, retaining high-resolution checkpoints around ingestion. A 60-second demo cannot establish digestion, glycogen replenishment or renal nutrient consequences. Derived from the executed saturation limits alone, 60 g carbohydrate cannot be digested faster than about 2.34 h and 20 g fat cannot be absorbed faster than about 2.92 h; concentration dependence makes actual times longer. These are source-rate lower bounds, not human observations. If the `/60` variant is tested, those digestion horizons change drastically.
3. **Meal versus fasting:** run 12–24 h from identical state with either scheduled meals or no caloric input, and a matched explicit hydration/sodium schedule. Evaluate glucose/hormone trajectories, glycogen, TAG/ketones, stored fat/protein, O2/CO2, renal water/substrate handling and fatigue. Lack of calorie actions is the fasting perturbation; deleting stores or editing blood glucose would be a different intervention. Baseline glycogen may start at its cap, so immediate liver-glycogen rise is not a universal acceptance criterion.
4. **Absorption block:** no public `SEConsumeNutrients` absorption-fraction action is present. Use an explicitly named source variant that multiplies the **paired** nutrient/sodium branch transfers in `AbsorbNutrients()` by a controlled fraction in [0,1], applying the same fraction to debit and credit; preserve digestion, sodium secretion, fluid/ion transport and the rest of the engine. Define whether the perturbation blocks carbohydrate only, all macro uptake, or water/ions as well. A zero-macro-uptake case should retain native chyme nutrients while reducing downstream nutrient delivery relative to the same meal. With no fecal path, blocked nutrients accumulate; this is an intestinal uptake ablation, not a complete malabsorption disease model. Do not erase nutrient masses after the step or edit aortic glucose to manufacture the expected curve.
5. **Recovery/rechallenge:** restore the uptake fraction and continue from blocked state; investigate clearance of accumulated chyme and delayed hormone/storage response. Compare action-free control and meal action at the same wall-clock-independent simulation times. Add exercise demand only after the standalone meal chain is observed, using the existing native exercise action to interrogate substrate availability and achieved work.

The current 3600 s adapter cap needs deliberate extension for these runs, or verified state-continuation chunks with globally consistent event times. A single continuous engine process is preferable until save/load continuity of all relevant metabolic history is checked. Keep the 0.02 s solver step; reducing CSV cadence does not justify coarsening integration. Upstream-versus-corrected calcium and renal variants, absorption ablation, and untouched control each require distinct provenance and output directories.

Acceptance should include causal ordering, local paired-transfer budgets, nonnegative states, explicit source-variant differences, checkpoint continuity, and independent meal/metabolic validation targets. The retained source supports a meaningful integrated substrate-control demonstration. It does not yet support the full human feeding-to-elimination mechanism implied by realistic alimentary anatomy.
