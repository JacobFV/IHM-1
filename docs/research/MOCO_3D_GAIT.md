# Native 3D contact gait frontier

The held OpenSim source includes a genuine three-dimensional contact-tracking example, `OpenSim/Examples/Moco/example3DWalking/example3DWalking.cpp`, at revision `86b30588374650fbaf012a345a836a64f6855522`. It is distinct from the planar SCONE gait and from the `exampleMocoTrack.cpp` example that prescribes measured external ground forces.

The source contact example prepares 22 bodies, 80 muscles, 33 coordinates and 12 foot-ground contact elements. Its held subject mass is approximately 85.2698 kg; the canonical body's 77.11 kg is a separate subject/registration assumption. The reference kinematics, ground forces, model, force sets and function-based muscle paths are retained from the primary repository. The model README identifies the Rajagopal/Uhlrich/Lai source lineage and AddBiomechanics preprocessing. No reference trajectory has been represented as a forward simulation.

## Concrete backend preflight

The existing `libosimMoco.so` was built with `OPENSIM_WITH_CASADI=OFF`. The native preflight reports `MocoCasADiSolver::isAvailable() == false` and exits 7. An isolated library compiled from the same source reports true and successfully initializes the contact model. The original OpenSim and SCONE libraries are preserved.

`scripts/build_native_moco3d.py` obtains the primary-maintained CasADi 3.6.5 aarch64 wheel, verifies its PyPI SHA-256 receipt, and uses its C++ headers/libraries. The cp311 Python extension is not imported into this Python 3.13 environment. This is the CasADi version pinned by the held OpenSim dependency configuration. Actual native linking, rather than wheel naming alone, establishes C++ compatibility. The builder replaces the backend object, adds the required CasOC objects and retains all other Moco objects. Its manifest records source hashes, object list, commands and library hashes.

## Source-faithful bounded experiment

The source first solves a torque-driven contact-tracking problem, then uses it as the initial guess for an 80-muscle problem. Both cover source times 0.48–1.61 s with 0.02 s mesh intervals and the source Legendre-Gauss-Radau-3 transcription, tracking weights, contact parameters, toe mechanics and solver tolerances. The muscle problem includes periodicity constraints. Source contact geometry receives its documented 2 cm adjustment; this is not an anatomical registration to the canonical body.

`scripts/run_native_moco3d.py` copies the exact model/data into a fresh artifact directory and records the wrapper patch. Changes add explicit stage selection, four solver threads, iteration/wall-time budgets, result status and a separate ODE forward rollout. Visualization is removed. A failed iterate is explicitly named and never promoted as a solution; a timed-out run remains incomplete.

The planned numerical acceptance requires solver success, finite optimized and forward trajectories over the full source interval, physical contact and muscle/actuator telemetry, and a forward comparison. Predeclared forward coordinate tolerances are maximum 5 cm translational and 0.1 rad angular differences from optimized states. These are engineering regression tolerances, not human-validation thresholds. Contact force and coordinate-actuator contributions must remain visible; they cannot be silently relabeled as muscle-generated support. The actual prepared torque plant has 25 coordinate actuators (upper-body optimal torque 50 Nm, toes 10 Nm, other leg joints 500 Nm), with no pelvis actuator. The six pelvis freedoms obtain support through contact. The neighboring measured-load example adds pelvis residuals, but this contact problem does not.

This work does not equate motion tracking, optimization convergence and closed-loop neural walking. The optimized control schedule is an open-loop trajectory; its independent forward rollout tests numerical realization, not robustness, cortical intent or a personalized controller.

## Actual collocation result and failed forward gate

`data/derived/locomotion/moco3d-torque-v2` converged after 339 iterations (approximately 631 solver seconds), with native `Solve_Succeeded` and unscaled constraint violation `2.2299128254132938e-5`. Its peak optimized vertical ground force was 959.988 N. The independent source ODE rollout completed the interval but **failed** the predeclared coordinate agreement gate: maximum pelvis tilt error was approximately 4.963 rad. This is a retained collocation candidate, not accepted executable walking.

The forward diagnosis preserves the actual optimized model and successful solution hashes in `moco3d-torque-forward-1e8`. Repeating the source rollout at integrator accuracy `1e-8`, rather than `1e-6`, still produced 4.790 rad pelvis tilt and 0.323 m vertical translation errors. Initial state transfer was exactly equal. All 25 scalar actuator overrides were disabled. Every recorded control matched interpolation of its optimized absolute-path control within `1.03e-7`. These checks rule out the observed failure being a gross initial-state, actuator-override or control-column mismatch; they do not establish the exact mathematical source of the instability.

The held `MocoUtilities.cpp:122` forward helper uses `PiecewiseLinearFunction`, initial states from the exact trajectory names and first row, and a 1 ms states reporter. `MocoDirectCollocationSolver.cpp:28` defaults interior control interpolation to true; `CasOCLegendreGaussRadau.cpp:74` enforces the corresponding linear control interpolation between mesh endpoints. Thus the forward interpolation convention is consistent with this source transcription. Small early deviations (~0.00004 rad at 0.5–0.6 s) grow to ~0.005 rad at 0.8 s and ~0.563 rad at 1.0 s, consistent with unstable open-loop propagation of small numerical defects. This is a diagnosis to test, not a claim of confirmed mesh convergence.

A separately labeled half-mesh (`0.01 s`) torque continuation completed its bounded experiment. The source muscle-driven stage completed its bounded attempt without convergence. The continuation changes numerical resolution and the initial guess only; it does not change contact properties, actuator strengths, tracking goals or acceptance limits. The muscle stage uses the source's torque-state guess transfer and zero control initialization. Neither optimization is accepted gait evidence. All failed solves and forward records remain retained.

The additional `moco3d-torque-v2/actuator_mapping_diagnosis.json` checks all 25 coordinate actuator outputs against `control * optimal_force` in both optimized and forward records. Every torque agrees within `1e-8 Nm`. Native actuator strengths and force application therefore match the prescribed control inventory. Negative acceptance tests in `moco3d-backend-acceptance/gait_negative_gates.json` reject backend-only initialization, the intentionally unsuccessful one-iteration muscle solve, and the converged torque candidate with failed forward agreement.

The full muscle stage (`moco3d-muscle-v1`) reached the explicit 1800 s wall-time budget after 1800.636 s. Its last logged iteration was 76, with unscaled primal residual approximately 45.2 and dual residual 1.83. It produced no successful muscle solution or forward rollout; gait acceptance correctly rejects it. Native intermediate trajectories at iterations 0, 25, 50 and 75 are retained with output hashes. This bounded result establishes an executable optimization backend and a difficult unresolved solve, not impossibility of solving the source problem.

The half-mesh continuation (`moco3d-torque-halfmesh-v1`) converged after 218 iterations, with 1438.537 s in IPOPT and 1449.655 s for the native process including rollout. Native solver status was `Solve_Succeeded`, unscaled constraint violation `5.1661758618593012e-5`, and process exit 0. The separate shell orchestration session later reported 143 despite the completed native execution receipt and output set; this does not replace the recorded native process status. The refined candidate also failed both unchanged forward gates: left ankle maximum error 28.326 rad, pelvis tilt 3.235 rad, pelvis rotation 4.270 rad. Optimized peak vertical ground force was 949.244 N. Refinement did not establish monotonic forward convergence or stable gait. It also reoptimized the control trajectory, so comparing individual endpoint errors alone is not a mesh-order estimate.

## Final bounded conclusion

The native CasADi/IPOPT backend and primary source contact model are executable, with fresh initialization and source/dependency receipts verified. Two torque collocation candidates converged but failed independent forward validation; the 80-muscle attempt exhausted its explicit 30-minute budget without convergence. No source-supported stabilizing 3D feedback controller was substituted, and no candidate is rendered or registered as working walking. A further research step would need demonstrated forward convergence or a documented stabilizing controller, then muscle-driven and subject-registration validation. The retained experiments do not justify changing the canonical mass, control gains, contact coefficients or gait acceptance thresholds.
