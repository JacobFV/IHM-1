## Human hair sampling evidence

[2017 human hair follicle density/touch study, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5451466/) uses cyanoacrylate skin surface samples and light microscopy. Its nine-site pretest has N=15. Regional mean ± SD follicles/cm²: forehead 285.0±84.1; neck 47.3±17.1; chest 28.4±6.4; upper arm 45.9±14.5; forearm 40.3±14.9; lower back 24.7±5.9; lower abdomen 16.9±6.2; thigh 21.0±8.0; calf 15.6±3.6. The main forearm cohort (N=55, mean 37.4±10.0, range 17–59) is distinct. The generator uses the coherent N=15 table; regional SD describes people, not a confidence probability for an atlas face. Each emitted follicle receives one hypothetical visible vellus shaft: growth phase, follicle units and terminal hairs are not resolved.

[1991 vellus hair study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1993141/) is a separate method/cohort, with forehead/back figures 439/85 per cm² confirmed in its abstract for comparison. Those values are not pooled or used as generator coefficients. Scalp, beard and terminal body hair need separate distribution evidence.

Geometry: BodyParts3D skin source, canonical frame x-left/y-superior/z-anterior in metres. Same-coordinate attribute seams are welded only within one entity for physical topology auditing. Input source face order remains unchanged for material attachments. There is no distance-based merge between anatomical entities. Region masks are explicit conservative coordinate priors; named somatic patch landmarks do not establish measured skin labels. Entire hands/feet are excluded to protect palms/soles. Groin, axilla, scalp and face except a narrow forehead mask remain excluded/unresolved. All triangle corners must agree on inclusion. The welded atlas skin is a thin shell (enclosed tissue volume about 0.00343 m³), not a whole-body solid. A conservative regional anatomical-axis outward-normal test (cosine >0.35) rejects inward shell faces and ambiguous tangent faces. This is an inferred filter, with possible omissions at nonconvex anatomy, not a measured outer epidermis segmentation. Zero density in unresolved masks does not mean biological absence of hair.

Follicles are independent Poisson samples with expectation triangle area × density, uniform barycentric positions, stable integer IDs in a pinned skin/seed namespace. Full samples are stored independently from deterministic display thinning. Radius 8–16 µm and shaft length 0.5–2 mm are explicit vellus morphology coefficient priors, not measurements from the density study. Polygonal tube radius uses physical units without amplification. The full sample archive retains roots, normals, radii, lengths and triangle/barycentric MaterialPoint coordinates. Rendering fewer shafts never changes full sample identity or anatomical density.

Deformation contract: evaluate barycentric coordinates against the same source triangle in the deformed skin vertex array; transport each shaft frame using that triangle's current tangents/normal. Geometry includes an attachment sidecar reference. This is a material attachment contract; native viewer nonrigid deformation requires consuming that metadata. Recompute flow resistances if vascular segment geometry deforms.

## Fine vascular transport evidence and synthesis

[Pries et al. 1992, blood viscosity in tube flow](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1481902/) concerns high-shear in-vitro data, diameter 3.3–1978 µm, discharge hematocrit up to 0.9. [Pries et al. 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7923637/) measured rat mesenteric networks, not human skin. The latter reports stronger in-vivo small-vessel resistance than glass-tube results. These establish why constant bulk viscosity is inappropriate as a validated capillary law. Neither paper establishes the present human forearm morphology, capillary density or a patient-specific boundary pressure.

The present solver deliberately implements a heterogeneous coefficient PRIOR, not an audited implementation of either Pries law. Apparent viscosity is 4.0 mPa s below diameter 12 µm, 2.4 at 12–30 µm, and 1.8 above/equal 30 µm. These are transparent synthesis parameters awaiting calibration. Hematocrit redistribution, RBC transit, shear response, glycocalyx, oxygen exchange and active autoregulation are absent. Resistance is 8 μ L/(π r⁴); sparse nodal conductance equations solve internal pressure with prescribed boundary pressures. Signed segment flow, branch residual, nonnegative dissipation and boundary-work equality are verified. This is a passive steady conductance network with no blood storage.

Eight bilateral forearm units each connect paired binary supply/return trees with 32 capillaries. Radii, lengths, spatial branching, unit count and 4500/1500 Pa supply/return pressures are explicit authored priors. Counts are an illustrative bounded materialization, not measured capillaries/mm². Each unit has an inferred named forearm cutaneous territory boundary; no nearest native artery is invented. The graph is connected internally, but native blood storage is explicitly unconnected. Coupling it to systemic transport requires identified terminal vessel/territory exchange ports and conservative interface integration.

Only explicit `--append` updates `data/derived/app/manifest.json`; neither invocation overwrites canonical `anatomy.json` nor `manifest_fragment.json`. The two geometry files are under its `geometry/` directory.

Verification commands: `scripts/verify_body_details.py`, `scripts/verify_body_hair.py`, `scripts/verify_body_vascular.py` under `.venv/bin/python`. They verify analytic tube flow, branching, paired capillary connectivity, pressure bounds, conservation, passivity/work equality, disconnected-boundary rejection, exact intra-entity welding, barycentric deformation and physical shaft radius. Source accuracy limitations remain separate from numerical success.
