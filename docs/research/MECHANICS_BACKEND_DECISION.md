# Respiratory coupling and mechanics backend evidence

The existing ARM64 BioGears build supports a real constitutive input through `BioGearsEngine.GetCircuits().GetActiveRespiratoryCircuit()`. The left/right `PleuralCavityToRespiratoryMuscle` paths accept `GetNextCompliance()` in L/cmH2O. The adapter partitions the specified total compliance using the stabilized native left/right fractions. Native pressure generation, fluid circuit solution, gas advection, blood gas exchange and control retain their original ownership.

This is a prescribed constitutive boundary, not full three-dimensional mechanics feedback. The separate canonical thoracic replay consumes the resulting gas volumes. Its three-mode constraint reaction is recorded but is not added to the native driver, which would double-count chest recoil without a replacement constitutive contract.

The real 12-second generic-profile comparison is retained under `data/derived/audits/respiratory-port/experiment-xl1lr93f/`. At total compliance 0.20 L/cmH2O, lung-volume excursion over samples after 4 seconds was 518.136448 mL; at 0.10 it was 351.699436 mL. Airway flow also changed. These are numerical intervention results, not patient calibration or steady-state tidal-volume estimates. Each run retains native CSV, port telemetry, states, configuration and execution hashes.

Setting the native respiratory muscle pressure before `AdvanceModelTime()` is unsuitable: `RespiratoryDriver()` overwrites it in preprocessing. A future reaction callback belongs immediately before the respiratory circuit `Process()` call; it must replace a specified constitutive contribution rather than append duplicate work. Mechanical ventilation is a separate supported mouth pressure/flow action and must not be described as chest-wall feedback.

Source anchors: preserved BioGears `Respiratory.cpp` around `PreProcess`, `Process`, `RespiratoryDriver`, and `UpdatePleuralCompliance`; `Controller/BioGears.cpp` respiratory circuit construction; `SECircuitManager.cpp` current/next mutability. Original physiological source bytes are unchanged. The thin IHM adapter adds circuit inputs and observational telemetry.

FEBio 4.13 now builds locally on ARM64 from pinned, unmodified source and passes a two-element analytical finite-strain compression benchmark. The [retained reference benchmark](REFERENCE_MECHANICS_BENCHMARK.md) records native outputs, executable/library hashes and the resolved OpenMP link failure. Its [rigid-wall contact benchmark](REFERENCE_CONTACT_BENCHMARK.md) also passes five native runs with open gap, indentation and release; penalty refinement reaches 0.011225% force error against a confined analytical limit. This does not establish equivalence to nonuniform forearm indentation. SOFA remains uninstalled. Heterogeneous anatomy, articulated joints and bed equilibrium remain unresolved gates.

The regional IHM tetrahedral solver now executes a frictionless, quasistatic forearm indentation study with 98,304 tetrahedra. Source-body face coordinates anchor this synthesized 10×10×3 mm reference volume. Its neo-Hookean shear prior transfers an apparent measurement from 20 older adults; thickness, compressibility and conduction delay remain priors. Actual 0.1 mm indentation refinement at lateral grid divisions 12,16,24,32 gave reactions 0.016857,0.017273,0.017781,0.017936 N; the last change is approximately0.87%. Earlier coarse results are nonmonotonic because the circular tool edge is represented by nodal constraints. This final observable meets the proposed1% successive-refinement check, not empirical validity or a proof of asymptotic convergence. The unconstrained unloaded reference is an exact quasistatic equilibrium; release does not model dynamic relaxation.

The affine whole-body solver and three-mode thorax remain reductions. Regional contact and IBM transfer results do not add duplicate mass or forces to them. Full all-organ contact and native 3D pressure/work feedback remain research work.
