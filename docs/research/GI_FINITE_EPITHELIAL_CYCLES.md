# Finite GI epithelial cycle preparation

`ihm/assembly/gi_epithelial_cycles.py` implements an independent lumen/cell/serosa preparation with five explicit species: Na+, K+, Cl−, neutral glucose and neutral amino acid. It reuses only `R`, `F` and the three-compartment incidence matrix from the skin electrodiffusion primitive. It does not import skin transport coefficients, geometry or voltage priors, and does not accept or mutate native BioGears stores.

The source-conditioned cycle columns are SGLT1 (2 Na + 1 glucose lumen→cell), neutral-AA B0AT1 (1 Na + 1 AA lumen→cell), Na/K pump (3 Na cell→serosa and 2 K serosa→cell), K recycling, nutrient efflux, and separate reversible paracellular Na and Cl paths. References and source applicability are retained in [the primary evidence audit](GI_ELECTROGENIC_TRANSPORT_AUDIT.md). The existing native positive lumped `AminoAcids` is deliberately not mapped to this neutral species.

Every application requests a finite molar extent, caps it jointly by all donors, and applies the same extent to every stoichiometric debit and credit. Floating-point rounding retreats the extent toward zero if needed, rather than clipping individual masses. Zero and exact exhaustion are permitted. This preserves each species across all three pools without adding counterions.

Two explicit capacitors join lumen–cell and cell–serosa, with serosa grounded. Fixed initial countercharge establishes the caller's supplied initial potentials. Subsequent ionic transfers change capacitor charge and voltage. No operation restores electroneutrality by inventing chloride or discarding charge. Chemical free energy uses ideal activities at fixed volumes and temperature, with the continuous zero-inventory limit; electrical energy uses the capacitor quadratic form.

An unpowered finite request is admitted only if its endpoint free-energy change is nonpositive. Pump requests consume a finite **external** ATP-cycle allowance at a caller-supplied hydrolysis free energy. They fail without changing state if their energy increase exceeds the supplied work. The difference between supplied work and free-energy increase is recorded as isothermal dissipation. Reverse ATP synthesis is explicitly unsupported. ATP, ADP and phosphate are not explicit chemical species here; this boundary therefore does not claim complete reaction chemistry or predict intracellular ATP concentration. The external allowance must eventually connect once to native energy ownership, not become an additional arbitrary metabolic debit.

This is an endpoint accounting fixture, not a time integrator or calibrated kinetic model. Endpoint energy decrease alone does not establish that every intermediate state follows a downhill physical trajectory; a future rate integrator must enforce instantaneous electrochemical forces and resolve stopping at equilibrium. Requested extents are interventions, not measured rates. Large requests can be rejected on energy grounds even with adequate donor mass. Test capacitances, volumes and ATP free energies are synthetic priors, not fits to human intestine. No current-density, absorption-rate, portal-delivery or pH prediction is warranted.

Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_gi_epithelial_cycles.py`. Bounded tests cover exact 2:1 transport, exhaustion and repeated zero donor use, sodium-limited pump extent, finite ATP exhaustion, rejected unfunded pump and uphill cotransport with unchanged state, reversible downhill paracellular sodium, mixed AA/pump conservation, global charge incidence and work/free-energy/dissipation closure. A scarce-donor synthetic pump case explicitly supplies 200 kJ/mol ATP work to test algebraic donor exhaustion; it is not a physiological ATP energy assertion. Other tests use 50 kJ/mol solely as a declared fixture prior.

Next implementation requires separate nutrient/ion activity models, measured intestinal kinetics and geometry, explicit time evolution, and coupled ATP/ADP/Pi and thermal ownership. Preserve this module as a bounded conservative reference while those interfaces are established. The native absorption correction remains unchanged.
