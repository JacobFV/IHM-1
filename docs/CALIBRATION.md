# Observation-level calibration

Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_calibration.py` and `.venv/bin/python scripts/calibrate_skin.py`. The latter reads `data/derived/integumentary/human-wound-observations.jsonl`, verifies the SHA256 of its referenced raw article, and writes `data/derived/calibration/skin-fit.json` plus a compact `index.json`. It does not modify raw observations or existing skin mechanism parameters.

## Bounded numerical fitting

`ihm.calibration.bounded_fit(predict, observed, sigma, initial, bounds, parameter_scale=...)` solves weighted residual least squares in dimensionless parameter coordinates. It validates finite observations, positive supplied error scales and bounded parameters, and fails on unsuccessful optimization. A three-point Jacobian SVD reports rank, singular values, conditioning, active bounds and unidentifiable directions in scaled coordinates. Its relative rank tolerance is 1e-8; parameter scales must reflect the caller's chosen physical scale. Scaling observation values and sigma together leaves the objective and rank unchanged; changing parameter units requires transforming initial values, bounds and parameter scales together.

For full rank and inactive bounds, the generic covariance is the inverse weighted information transformed back to physical parameter units. It assumes known independent Gaussian observation errors and local linearity; it is not multiplied by a guessed residual variance. Rank-deficient fits and active constraints return null covariance rather than false finite precision for unidentifiable directions. Singular vectors record which combinations cannot be resolved. These local diagnostics cannot establish global or structural identifiability for an arbitrary nonlinear model.

## Human wound-field phenotype

The real data are the [human wound-field study, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3228273/#T1). The existing extraction contains 160 site observations for 40 people, four age/sex groups and four control/wound sites. Raw source SHA256 is checked against every row's provenance before materialization. All rows remain in output, including the ten missing or author-excluded cells; their raw values, SEM, original labels, discrepancy notes and exclusion reasons are preserved.

A subject key combines age group, sex and participant row. The supplied extraction aligns that row across sites; no independently released subject identifier exists, so this linkage assumption remains explicit. Participant numbers divisible by five form the fixed holdout in each group. All observations from one subject stay together. The materialized split has 32 training subjects (121 usable rows) and eight holdout subjects (29 usable rows). Missing cells do not change the split. The young group is labeled 18–29 in the table but 18–25 in methods/abstract; both are retained as an unresolved source discrepancy.

The phenotype is an additive mean model: intercept + older age group + female + LA-1 + LA-2 + LL-1 indicators. Control, young and male are the reference categories. There are six full-rank coefficients, all in V/m, bounded numerically at ±1,000 V/m. These broad bounds are not biological priors. The model estimates demographic/site associations, not effects of interventions or healing dynamics. It can produce slightly negative group means and interval endpoints; those expose an unconstrained additive approximation rather than a claim of valid nonnegative amplitude distributions. No clipping conceals that limitation.

Each included observation receives equal weight. The per-person SEM is based on usually three repeated scans, with exact cell-specific count unavailable. It does not describe population dispersion and cannot provide inverse-variance population precision. It is preserved in the record but never substituted for between-person SD. The generic solver's covariance is explicitly discarded for this fit. Uncertainty instead uses a subject-cluster sandwich covariance with CR1 finite-sample correction and Student t quantiles with 31 degrees of freedom. This allows residual dependence among sites within a person. Mean prediction intervals use that coefficient covariance; approximate individual intervals add pooled training residual variance. Neither interval assumes the scan SEM represents biological heterogeneity. Heteroscedasticity across sites/groups, small cluster count and unmodeled interactions limit interval accuracy.

`predict_skin(result, age_group, sex, site)` returns mean, mean SE, mean interval and approximate individual interval in V/m. It rejects categories outside those actually observed. Hyperparameters and split are fixed before holdout evaluation. Reported holdout errors remain separate from training and no final refit absorbs holdout subjects.

## Observable binding

A measured `mV/mm` equals one `V/m`; a `mV` is a voltage and is rejected by the field converter. `lateral_field(surface_potential_v, positions_m, edges)` evaluates signed `E_ab=(phi_a-phi_b)/distance_m`. `skin_surface_field(model, patch)` reads only the existing model's apical extracellular potential states, matching `ihm.materialize.skin.electric_field`. Cell membrane potentials are excluded. This binds the correct physical quantity but does not supply subject-matched electrode coordinates, wound geometry or an observation kernel; the phenotype's aggregate field cannot be directly equated to an arbitrary synthetic edge or its microscopic membrane voltage.

The human data therefore calibrate a phenotypic field predictor, while the existing skin electrical model retains illustrative conductances, pumps and reservoirs. Many conductance/pump combinations can produce the same extracellular field. The generic rank machinery supports testing a future mechanism fit, but this dataset cannot identify all channel conductances or certify cell-level dynamics.

## Materialized result and checks

Training RMSE is 35.35 V/m. On the eight held-out people, RMSE is 34.86 V/m and MAE 31.05 V/m; a training-mean baseline has RMSE 46.09 V/m. Mean holdout prediction bias is +11.63 V/m. These are modest same-study generalization results, not external clinical validation. Coefficients are approximately intercept 45.86, older −46.42, female +12.81, LA-1 +61.42, LA-2 +79.91 and LL-1 +74.03 V/m.

The verification script checks known synthetic parameter recovery, output/sigma unit invariance, rank-deficient ambiguity, physical field conversion, rejection of voltage-as-field, real-data split separation, preservation of all 160 rows, unchanged coefficients after altering only holdout outcomes, finite predictive uncertainty and equality with the existing skin surface gradient operator. JSON serialization rejects NaN/Infinity. Source hashes and the fixed split are saved with model results.
