# Native postural control experiments

These controllers deliver muscle excitations to freely integrated OpenSim dynamics. They do not prescribe coordinates, tether the pelvis, or train the IBM brain. Forward displacement during a fall is not walking.

`ihm/native/postural_control.py` provides normalized fiber length and velocity feedback. `scripts/evaluate_native_posture.py` compares feedback, identical constant baselines, and zero commands. `scripts/train_native_posture.py` performs seeded bounded parameter search with native opaque checkpoints. Its objective rewards upright survival and penalizes pelvis tilt, roll, and speed; it does not reward distance traveled by falling.

`ihm/native/moment_arm_control.py` differentiates the retained polynomial muscle paths, computes bounded torque allocation, and applies joint and vestibular feedback. Native geometric muscle moment arms can supplement the fitted leg paths. Strength allocation is approximate: Gaussian force-length scaling omits pennation, tendon compliance, passive force, force-velocity, and activation lag. Residual requested torque remains observable.

The source plant has 80 leg muscles. The ordinary Arm26 augmentation has 92 muscles and still lacks lumbar actuation. In the initial native upright joint-feedback trial, the lumbar coordinate collapsed to -2.55 radians by 1.06 s; leg-only feedback cannot independently stabilize this coordinate. Both default and higher joint feedback gains failed the 0.6 m pelvis-height cutoff around 1.1 s. The retained 98-muscle lumbar variant adds six donor erector-spinae/oblique paths and is being evaluated separately.

Native experiment directories retain assembled-model/build identities, full coordinate trajectories, commands, torque allocation residuals, and contact observations. `walking_demonstrated` and `brain_trained` remain false for these engineering controller trials. Separate kernel motor-learning evidence belongs to `IBM_NATIVE_MOTOR_LEARNING.md`.

Current canonical metadata differed from the historical lumbar artifact's source hashes. `scripts/rematerialize_current_lumbar.py` regenerates the audit and explicitly requires unchanged mechanical registration, candidate muscle parameters, and insertion XML before retaining the native model bytes. The fresh registration is `data/derived/mechanics/whole_body_lumbar_current/registration.json`; this avoids accepting a stale manifest or silently blessing changed mechanics.

The initial engineering vestibular ankle sign was also rejected by an independent native joint-axis audit. The ankle's parent is the tibia and child is the talus; with a planted foot, positive ankle torque rotates the shank toward positive ground X. Positive pelvis tilt tips the upper body toward negative ground X, so the corrective ankle contribution is positive. The controller now tests this sign explicitly. Failed candidate trajectories retain their original policy/commands and must not be interpreted as evaluations of later corrections.

The 98-muscle corrected-sign candidate held the lumbar angle near neutral but still fell at 1.37 s; COM feedback extended that to 1.54 s. The corresponding 86-muscle search variant (same 22 inertial bodies, 80 legs plus six lumbar muscles) reproduced the 1.54 s fall while avoiding the cost of 12 Arm26 wrapped paths. This is a speed experiment, not the default integrated human model.

A native initial-state measurement then found the COM at X=-0.0964 m and the foot load-weighted support center at X=+0.0210 m, an 11.74 cm backward offset. Targeting the original COM therefore tried to retain an unstable standing geometry. Explicit support-center targeting and a physically balanced initial-state solve are separate subsequent candidates, not retroactive claims that earlier controls worked.

`initial_stance86` and `initial_stance98` provide statically aligned startup candidates: native ground contact sums to 686.7000003 N at 70 kg and COM/load-center X mismatch is 1.3e-11 m. The original zero-angle startup instead carried about 38,952 N before integration. This initialization correction removes a contact shock; it does not imply equilibrium of every muscle/joint or stable standing. Dynamic candidates still fall.

A matched-checkpoint native pulse test measured the immediate COM response to bilateral ±20 Nm ankle requests: +20 increases COM X velocity by 0.00782 m/s, −20 decreases it by 0.01037 m/s after 50 ms. The local feedback sign is supported by this experiment, while longer contact/posture coupling remains unresolved. See `scripts/verify_native_ankle_pulse.py` and its retained receipt.

The copied-State static query now supports upright activation overrides and reports actual clamped activation, generalized residuals, and the native dynamic identity `r + M * udot`. Replay checks in upright, supine, and free environments found continuing trajectories unchanged; residual-identity errors were below 7e-13.

Solving 86 muscle activations reduced the 13 controlled leg/lumbar residuals from 30.24 Nm to 0.000164 Nm. It exposed excluded initial torques of roughly 49.4 Nm at each elbow and 7.53 Nm at each MTP. The retained elbow passive law produces about 48.8 Nm at its zero default angle. Therefore even a contact-balanced pose with leg/lumbar equilibrium can start with a substantial unactuated arm/toe transient. Full initial-state equilibrium and sustained balance remain separate acceptance conditions.

Refined resting muscle/posture initialization plus the corrected lateral law extended an 86-muscle native feedback trajectory to 7.56 s before a sagittal fall. A matched held-excitation trajectory fell at 1.69 s. These results support a causal stabilization effect, not sustained standing. A 5 N, 100 ms diagonal pelvis perturbation still led to a fall at 6.15 s with another candidate.

An independent full native linearization includes 234 state variables (independent coordinates and speeds, activations, and fiber lengths) and 86 excitation inputs. Continuous LQR stabilized the local linear model but its 10 ms sampled nonlinear trial clipped many muscle commands and fell at 6.71 s. Sampled-data controller design and actual nonlinear perturbation acceptance are required; a stable continuous eigenvalue is not runtime evidence.

The 10 ms sampled system exposed a discrete spectral radius of 16.57 for the continuous controller. Exact zero-order-hold discretization and a discrete Riccati solve removed that instability. Uniform input penalties still failed a push because several equilibrium muscle activations sit close to the native minimum activation of 0.01; a command above zero can remain below that physical floor. The controller now distinguishes physical-floor violations from wire clipping.

A margin-aware discrete controller on the 70 kg, 86-muscle refined resting plant completed a 10 s native trial with a 5 N forward plus 5 N lateral pelvis force applied for 100 ms at t=1 s. Peak COM displacement was 1.031 mm; final horizontal COM displacement was 56 micrometers and final COM speed was 1.91e-7 m/s. Commands did not clip at wire bounds. The matched held-equilibrium arm fell at 2.75 s. Receipts are `data/research/locomotion_control/lqr86_margin_push/report.json` and `lqr86_held_push/report.json`. This supports local engineered stance recovery for that perturbation, plant, mass, and horizon. It does not establish walking, cortical control, or robustness to larger disturbances. Full 98-muscle and patient-mass transfer are separate tests.

The full 98-muscle plant independently completed the same 10 s perturbation trial: peak COM displacement 1.371 mm, final horizontal displacement 52 micrometers, and final speed 1.78e-7 m/s. Some transient commands crossed the actual 0.01 activation floor, but the native system recovered. The receipt and complete muscle-state trajectory are retained in `lqr98_margin_push`. This remains a 70 kg local stance experiment; patient-mass transfer is being solved independently.

Correction to the preceding push descriptions: the native force API expects a ground-space point. Those trials supplied the numerical local pelvis COM tuple, so their applied forces had an unintended lever arm. Recovery remains an observed response to the retained actual forces, but these are not COM-centered push tests. Original evidence is unchanged and each trial has a correction note. The evaluator now queries the current ground-space pelvis COM every force step; patient-mass acceptance is repeated with this correction.

At the actual patient mass of 77.6122029 kg, the activation-only model variant preserves canonical BodySet, JointSet, coordinate defaults, and reference registration. An explicit 19-coordinate initial pose is applied once. Fresh native identification and the same 10 ms margin-aware design completed the corrected COM-centered 5 N forward + 5 N lateral, 100 ms push test for 10 s. Peak COM displacement was 1.706 mm; final horizontal displacement was 76 micrometers, and speed was 7.96e-8 m/s. Held equilibrium excitation with the same perturbation failed the posture cutoff at 2.89 s. See `patient98_margin_com_push` and `patient98_held_com_push`. The Riccati residual is ill-conditioned (0.415) with three nearly neutral modes; the retained nonlinear behavior supports this bounded recovery observation, not a general stability or optimality proof.

The promotable runtime artifact is `data/models/engineering_stance_v1`. It includes the activation-only native model, 98-muscle catalog, 10 ms policy, explicit initial pose and excitation maps, source provenance, and compressed corrected paired trajectories. Runtime dependencies resolve inside the bundle except explicit live canonical source checks. `EngineeredLQRStanceController` enforces the artifact model, mass, sampling interval, full native catalog, and rollback identity. Any sensory block disables privileged stance feedback; regional cortical motor output is recorded separately and inactive in this engineering mode. The native adapter smoke test covers the promoted artifact, sensory and motor blocks, and checkpoint restoration.

Gait references need separate source auditing: the retained coordinate file labels radians globally but its two dependent knee-beta channels are in degrees of the primary knee angles. The source model enforces beta=primary. The loader now excludes enforced dependent coordinates, yielding 23 independent non-root targets; it does not silently convert contradictory source metadata. This correction concerns target construction, not proof of native gait.

An independent opposite-direction COM push (-5 N forward and -5 N lateral for 100 ms) also recovered for 10 s with the unchanged promoted policy. Peak COM displacement was 1.889 mm; final horizontal displacement was 76 micrometers and speed was 9.16e-8 m/s. The held-out receipt and compressed trajectory are retained separately in `data/models/engineering_stance_v1_validation`, preserving the original promoted bundle identity.

For cortical-teacher development, an unchanged-state-penalty design with 1000 times the input penalty reduces feedback bandwidth. Its exact linear four-tick-delay system has no nonneutral mode outside the unit circle; the original fast gain does. The slower design independently completed the actual native COM-push test with a 40 ms queued command delay for 10 s: peak COM displacement 2.041 mm, final displacement 0.201 mm, and final speed 3.26e-6 m/s. No commands crossed either wire bounds or native activation floors. Evidence is `patient_slow_lqr_delay4_com_push`. A pure command queue is not identical to processing through an E/I circuit; actual cortical training and severed comparisons remain separate.

Moving toward gait, directly applying donor joint-reference offsets failed even at small amplitudes. A separate native static solve then produced an 80% left-foot load endpoint, including equilibrium activations and fiber lengths. Interpolating complete equilibrium references and endpoint gains through muscle excitation completed a 10 s actual native transfer: final left load 79.22%, COM speed 1.75e-5 m/s, no wire clipping, and positive contact-support proxy margin throughout. The body coordinates remained freely integrated. The original fixed-gain version drifted; only the scheduled-gain version settled. Evidence is `patient_weight_transfer80_scheduled`. Both feet remained in contact, so this is weight transfer, not walking.

The actual muscle-driven lift/swing program completed 22 s. Right-foot minimum clearance crossed zero at 8.73 s, then remained positive throughout the forward swing. The foot moved 145.6 mm forward and settled at 30.94 mm clearance; final COM speed was 3.85e-8 m/s. Left-foot horizontal drift was about 4.7 mm. These are directly observed contact-sphere positions from the continuing native process, not a reconstruction or prescribed path. The receipt is `patient_right_swing_program`, with `movement_summary.json` distinguishing swing from walking.

The first appended landing candidate failed at 24.13 s, before completing touchdown, and is not accepted. The retained failed trajectory shows growing right-hip errors and saturation. Stable endpoint linearizations do not guarantee stability while interpolating an airborne pose into contact. Footprint-preserving intermediate equilibrium states are being prepared; the previously accepted standing, weight transfer, and airborne swing remain separate evidence.
